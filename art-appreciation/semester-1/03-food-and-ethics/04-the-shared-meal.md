# Lesson 4: The Shared Meal — Art, Faith, and Community

**Today's goal:** See how meals define community. Use Berger's ideas to read images of ritual, home, and public life.

## Works

1. Leonardo da Vinci, *The Last Supper* (1495–1498)
2. Pieter Bruegel the Elder, *The Peasant Wedding* (1567)
3. Jean-François de Troy, *Luncheon with Oysters* (1735)
4. Jan Steen, *The Merry Family* (1668)
5. William Hogarth, *Marriage à-la-mode: The Tête à Tête* (1743)
6. Zhang Zeduan, *Along the River During the Qingming Festival* (12th c.)

## Key ideas from John Berger

- "The sharing of bread was once a pledge that all would live."
- "To eat together is to affirm the common necessity of life."

---

## Lesson Map

### 1) Introduction: The Ethics of Sharing Food
- **Question:** What does sharing food represent?
- **Goal:** Introduce food as a moral act that builds community.
- **Link to Berger:** "The sharing of bread was once a pledge that all would live."

### 2) Sacred Meals and Rituals
- **Artworks:** *The Last Supper* (Leonardo), *Supper at Emmaus* (Caravaggio)
- **Question:** How do sacred meals use food to convey divine order or moral responsibility?

### 3) The Domestic Meal: Community and Care
- **Artworks:** *The Peasant Wedding* (Bruegel), *Luncheon with Oysters* (de Troy)
- **Question:** How do domestic meals reveal values of family and class?

### 4) Critiquing Excess and Decay
- **Artworks:** *The Merry Family* (Steen), *Marriage à-la-mode* (Hogarth)
- **Question:** What happens when food becomes a symbol of excess or moral decay?

### 5) Public Meals and Shared Society
- **Artwork:** *Along the River During the Qingming Festival* (Zhang Zeduan)
- **Question:** How do public meals reflect social order and shared culture?

### 6) Conclusion: What Makes a "Good" Shared Meal?
- **Key Question:** What do these works suggest about the ethics of shared meals?
