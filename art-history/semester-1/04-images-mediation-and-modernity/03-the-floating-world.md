# 3. The Floating World: Ukiyo-e and the Question of Aura

*(Berger + Benjamin + Japanese woodblock prints)*

## Overview

We use **John Berger's Ways of Seeing (Chapter 1)** and Walter Benjamin to think about how images change when they are reproduced, focusing on **ukiyo-e woodblock prints** as an early example of mass-produced art that travelled across time and cultures.

## Topics Covered

- Ukiyo-e as a genre: floating world images, popular subjects (kabuki actors, courtesans, landscapes), and prints made in multiples
- How woodblock printing allowed images to circulate widely in Edo-period Japan
- The later reception of ukiyo-e in Europe (e.g. Impressionism) as an example of images gaining new meanings in new contexts
- Berger's argument that reproduction changes the "message" of an image
- Benjamin's ideas of **aura** and **mechanical reproduction**, read through ukiyo-e rather than only Western paintings

## Key Questions

- How does it change an artwork when it is designed from the start to be printed in many copies?
- What happens to the "aura" of an image when it moves from Edo Japan to European collectors to today's textbooks and screens?
- How do Berger's "ways of seeing" help us understand the journey of ukiyo-e images across cultures and formats?

## Key Concepts

- Ways of seeing (Berger)
- Aura (Benjamin)
- Original vs copy in print culture
- Mechanical reproduction (early: woodblock; later: photography)
- Cross-cultural circulation of images
