# 1. Introduction: From Shared Meals to Shared Images

## Overview

This lesson introduces the new unit by moving from **lived events** to **images**.

First, we revisit Rirkrit Tiravanija's shared meal artworks and the film *My Dinner with Andre* to see how ordinary situations can be structured like art and performance. Then we turn to **John Berger, Ways of Seeing (Chapter 1)** to explore how every image is a way of seeing, and how reproduction and context change what images mean.

---

## Topics Covered

- Rirkrit Tiravanija's shared meal works as artworks, social situations, and images
- *My Dinner with Andre* as a filmed conversation between life and performance
- Hyperreality (Baudrillard) and paratheatre (Grotowski)
- John Berger, *Ways of Seeing* Chapter 1
- Reproduction, context, and "ways of seeing"

---

## Key Questions

- When does an ordinary event (a meal, a conversation) become a kind of artwork or performance?
- How do hyperreality and paratheatre help describe these blurred situations?
- What does Berger mean by "ways of seeing"?
- How does the reproduction of images (in books, screens, social media) change their meaning?
- How can Berger's ideas help us understand both artworks and everyday images in our own lives?

---

## Key Concepts

- **Hyperreality** – A condition in which reality and image overlap and reinforce each other, so that it becomes hard to separate what is "real" from its representation.
- **Paratheatre** – Grotowski's term for experimental work that tries to dissolve the boundary between stage and everyday life, involving participants directly.
- **Relational artwork** – Art that uses social interaction, gatherings, or shared activities as its main material.
- **Ways of seeing** – Berger's idea that images are never neutral; each image expresses a particular point of view and invites a specific way of looking.
- **Reproduction** – The copying and circulation of images (through printing, photography, film, digital media), which changes their context and meaning.

---

## Key Berger Lines (Chapter 1)

- "Seeing comes before words. The child looks and recognizes before it can speak."
- "The way we see things is affected by what we know or what we believe."
- "An image is a sight which has been recreated or reproduced."
- "Every image embodies a way of seeing."
- "When the camera reproduces a painting, it destroys the uniqueness of its image. As a result its meaning changes."
