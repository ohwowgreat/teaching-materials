# Film Screening — La Chimera (Rohrwacher, 2023)

This lesson sits between Part 2 and Part 4. You have the vocabulary of patronage, the Church as institutional patron, and the idea that art is made inside a system of power. Now you are going to watch a film about what happens when objects are ripped out of that system entirely — and what happens to them next.

---

## The Film

**La Chimera** — dir. Alice Rohrwacher | Italy, 2023 | 130 min | Italian with subtitles

Arthur is a British archaeologist drifting through rural Italy sometime in the 1980s. He has a strange gift: he can feel, almost physically, where Etruscan tombs lie underground. He falls in with a group of *tombaroli*, illegal tomb robbers working for a local dealer named Spartaco, who sells whatever they find to collectors and intermediaries somewhere higher up the chain.

---

## Background — The Objects

The objects in the film are props, but every type shown is based on real Etruscan burial goods. The Etruscans were a civilization of central Italy, centered in what is now Tuscany and Lazio, who flourished between roughly the 9th and 1st centuries BCE. They buried their dead with objects meant to accompany them: pottery, bronzes, jewelry, terracotta figures.

Types you will see in the film:
- **Bucchero pottery** — a distinctive black-fired ceramic unique to Etruscan culture
- **Painted terracotta** — funerary urns, sarcophagus lids, and votive figures
- **Bronze objects** — candelabra, mirrors, and vessels
- **Gold jewelry** — fibulae, necklaces, and earrings

---

## Background — The Real Case

The supply chain Rohrwacher shows is closely based on the real operation run by **Giacomo Medici**, whose network was described in court as one of the largest smuggling rings ever uncovered. In September 1995, Italian and Swiss police raided a warehouse at the **Geneva Freeport** registered to Medici's company. Inside they found over ten thousand artifacts estimated at $35 million, including thousands of **polaroid photographs** showing the objects fresh from the ground — the central evidence linking them to their illegal excavation.

Medici was convicted in 2004 and sentenced to ten years in prison and a fine of €10 million. Evidence from Geneva was then used to charge **Robert Hecht**, an American dealer, and **Marion True**, the curator of antiquities at the J. Paul Getty Museum. The Getty returned 40 objects to Italy in 2007. The Metropolitan Museum of Art returned the **Euphronios Krater** to Italy in 2008.

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Tombaroli** | Italian for tomb robbers; typically local men paid small amounts by dealers to do the physical excavation |
| **Freeport** | A tax-free international storage zone where art can be held, bought, and sold with minimal oversight |
| **Provenance** | The documented ownership history of an object; absence of provenance is a warning sign the market often ignores |
| **Laundering** | The process of disguising an object's illegal origin by passing it through enough transactions that a clean paper trail replaces the real one |
| **Repatriation** | The legal and diplomatic process of returning cultural objects to their country of origin |

## Viewing Questionnaire

**1. Patronage as contract**
The *tombaroli* extract objects made for a specific ritual purpose. What happens to that original meaning the moment the object enters the market?

**2. Access and control**
Spartaco controls the flow of objects from the ground to the buyer. What specific actions give him that control?

**3. Labor and ownership**
Arthur finds the objects but does not profit from them. What does the film say about the relationship between discovery and ownership?

**4. Provenance and narrative**
The objects will eventually reach a collector or institution far from the field. What provenance story will follow them, and what will be omitted?

**5. The film's argument**
By the end of the film, what do you think Rohrwacher wants you to feel about the art market? Point to a specific scene.

---

## Connections to the Unit

- **Part 1** — The Etruscan objects had a patron: a community and a ritual. Removing them breaks the original contract of meaning.
- **Part 4** — Spartaco is a primary market dealer without the white walls. He controls access, price, and narrative.
- **Part 5** — Provenance is constructed at every stage. The polaroid photographs are the break in the chain the market tries to hide.
