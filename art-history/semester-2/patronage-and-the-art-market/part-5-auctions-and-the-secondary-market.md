# Part 5: Auctions and the Secondary Market

## Overview

The secondary market is where artworks circulate as objects with histories and claims attached to them. Unlike the primary market, where galleries set prices through relationships and positioning, the secondary market makes price public. Auctions are the mechanism. Catalogues, provenance, estimates, and the room itself are all part of how that price is constructed before the hammer falls.

> "Public auction records remain the only objective, publicly available source of individual transaction-level data in the art market."
> — Art Basel and UBS Global Art Market Report 2026

**By the end of the week, students should be able to:**
- Define estimate, reserve, guarantee, hammer price, buyer's premium, comps, provenance, and condition report

- Explain how comparable sales function as evidence in valuation logic
- Produce an estimate range with a written justification
- Separate object qualities from market storytelling

## Assessments

**Formative: Catalogue Annotation**
One annotated lot page identifying credibility signals and persuasion language. Due at the start of Class 3.

**Summative: Auction Catalogue Entry**
You are a specialist at a major auction house preparing a lot for an upcoming evening sale. Your job is to write the catalogue entry for a work by an artist of your choosing. The work can be real or hypothetical, but the artist must be real and must have a documented market presence.

Your entry must include:
- **Lot Header** — artist name, nationality, birth year; title, date, medium, dimensions; provenance (one to three lines); exhibition history (optional)
- **Catalogue Essay** (250–350 words) — authoritative, comparative, historically situated; make a case for the work, not a summary of the artist's career
- **Estimate Range** — low and high in USD, calibrated to the artist's real market tier
- **Valuation Rationale** (200–250 words) — at least two comparable sales with full details; one data point from the 2026 UBS Art Market Report; one risk note on each side of the estimate

Total length: approximately 500–600 words plus the lot header.

## Market Context

The 2026 UBS report documents a market returning to growth after two years of decline. Public auction sales rose 9% in 2025 to $20.7 billion, driven almost entirely by the high end.

Key data:
- Works sold for more than $1 million made up less than 1% of transactions but over 54% of total value
- Works under $50,000 made up 95% of all lots but only 15% of total value
- The market is structurally two different markets operating at the same time

## Reading

**Art Basel and UBS Global Art Market Report 2026**, Dr. Clare McAndrew, Arts Economics (free download at artbasel.com).

Read before Class 1:
- The introduction and global overview
- The auction sector section
- Any section covering collector behavior or buying motivations

**Also recommended:** William J. Baumol, *Unnatural Value: Or Art Investment as Floating Crap Game* — a concise argument about investment logic and market irrationality.

Students should read the UBS report as both a data source and a persuasion object. The report is produced in collaboration with UBS — a financial institution with deep interests in the art market. Notice what it counts, what it foregrounds, and whose voice it cites.
