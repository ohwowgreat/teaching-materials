# 4. In Defense of the Poor Image

## Overview

These lessons explore Hito Steyerl's idea of the **"poor image"** — low-resolution, compressed, repeatedly copied images that circulate online. The focus is on how image quality, circulation, and platform shape meaning. Steyerl's essay *In Defense of the Poor Image* is read alongside scenes from **Jean-Luc Godard's *Goodbye to Language*** and examples drawn from everyday media use (memes, screenshots, fan edits, reposted photos).

---

## Topics Covered

- Steyerl's concept of the **poor image** and its place in contemporary visual culture
- The relationship between **resolution**, **compression**, and perceived value
- How images move across platforms (feeds, chats, reposts) and change as they circulate
- Connections between poor images and scenes from *Goodbye to Language*
- The role of **medium and platform** in shaping how images are seen and understood
- Applying **Berger (ways of seeing)** and **McLuhan (medium is the message)** to everyday digital images

---

## Key Questions

- What is a "poor image," and why might someone defend it rather than dismiss it?
- How do images change — technically and in meaning — as they are compressed, cropped, re-captioned, and re-posted?
- In what ways do scenes from *Goodbye to Language* behave like poor images, and what does that do to our perception?
- How do different platforms (TikTok, Instagram, WeChat, group chats) change the "message" of the same image?
- How can ideas from Steyerl, McLuhan, and Berger help make sense of the images encountered every day online?

---

## Key Concepts

- **Poor image** – A low-resolution, compressed, often pirated or repeatedly saved image that loses visual quality but gains speed, reach, and visibility.
- **Resolution** – The degree of sharpness or detail in an image; how clear or pixelated it appears.
- **Circulation** – The paths images take as they move across platforms, communities, and contexts.
- **Medium / Platform** – The technical and social environment in which an image appears, which shapes how it is produced, shared, and read.
- **Ways of seeing (Berger)** – The idea that images always embody a point of view, and that their meaning shifts with context and arrangement.
- **The medium is the message (McLuhan)** – The claim that the structure and form of a medium have deeper effects on perception and society than any individual piece of content.
