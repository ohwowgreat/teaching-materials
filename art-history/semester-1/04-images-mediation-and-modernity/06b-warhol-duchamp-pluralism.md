# 6b. Warhol, Duchamp, and Pluralism — What "Ends" and What Comes After

## Overview

This lesson explains what Danto thought he saw in 1964 when he encountered Warhol's *Brillo Boxes*. The point is not that art disappears. The point is that art no longer has one clear direction or one shared set of standards. Instead, art becomes deeply tied to interpretation, context, and meaning. Danto describes this as the "end of art history" in the sense of a single storyline.

## Key Ideas

- Danto uses **Hegel's dialectic** as a way to think about progress in history: ideas and styles move through conflict and change.
- In traditional art history, movements can be described with shared goals and rubrics (Renaissance, Baroque, Impressionism, Cubism).
- Warhol's *Brillo Boxes* look like ordinary store cartons. This forces the question: If it looks the same, why is it art?
- Duchamp's readymades push the same problem earlier: an ordinary object becomes art through choice and framing.
- Danto argues that at this point art becomes something like **philosophy**: it asks questions about meaning, value, and categorization.
- After this "end," the art world becomes **pluralist**: many approaches exist at once, without a single dominant direction.

## What You Will Do in Class

- Review the key terms from Lesson 1.
- Learn the basic structure of a dialectic (thesis, antithesis, synthesis).
- Study Warhol and Duchamp as artists who force the definition problem.
- Practice explaining how context changes meaning (store vs museum, ordinary object vs art object).
- Discuss what "pluralism" feels like: freedom, confusion, or both.
- Look at contemporary examples where major artists do very different kinds of work, but are equally important in museums.

## Terms to Know

- **Dialectic**: a process where ideas change through conflict and response (thesis → antithesis → synthesis).
- **Readymade**: an ordinary object presented as art to force the question of what art is.
- **Pluralism**: no single style or movement is "the direction" of art; multiple approaches coexist.

## Takeaway

Danto's "end of art" is an end of a single storyline. After that, art becomes more open, more philosophical, and harder to judge using one shared rubric.
