# Images, Mediation, and Modernity

In this unit we will look at moments in art history when art and everyday reality start to look almost the same. We will ask how the ability to copy and share images (from prints and photographs to films and phone screens) has changed the way art appears and the way we see the world.

We will move from early photography and prints, through modern works that look like simple shapes or supermarket products, all the way to low-resolution internet images and museum Instagram feeds. Along the way, we will use ideas from writers like Walter Benjamin, John Berger, Susan Sontag, Arthur Danto, Jean Baudrillard, Vilém Flusser, Hito Steyerl, and Amelia Groom.

## What this unit is about

- How copying images changes what art looks like.
- Times when **art and non-art look the same**, and only context or theory tells us which is which.
- How image technologies (print, photography, film, digital) change the way we see, remember, and believe.
- Why art and images look the way they do **now**, and what that means for us.

## Guiding Questions

- How does the reproduction of images change what counts as an "original" artwork?
- When do art and reality become visually hard to tell apart?
- How do museums, screens, brands, and algorithms shape what we see every day?
- Why do art and images look the way they do now, and how does that affect how we live and see ourselves?

## Readings

- Walter Benjamin – *The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction*
- John Berger – *Ways of Seeing*
- Susan Sontag – "In Plato's Cave" (*On Photography*)
- Arthur Danto – "The End of Art" (Brillo Boxes)
- Jean Baudrillard – texts on simulacra and hyperreality
- Vilém Flusser – *Towards a Philosophy of Photography*
- Hito Steyerl – "In Defense of the Poor Image"
- Amelia Groom – "Permanent Collection"

*(You will only read 1–2 of these in full. We will work with selected passages for others in class.)*
