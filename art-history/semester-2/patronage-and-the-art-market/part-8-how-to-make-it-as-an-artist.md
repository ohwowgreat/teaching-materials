# Part 8: How to Make It as an Artist

## Overview

A serious artistic life requires professional competence. This unit converts the market literacy built across Parts 4 through 7 into a real submission. Students select a live opportunity aligned to their own practice and produce a complete, submission-ready packet. The work is judged on clarity, coherence, feasibility, and professionalism. The constraints are real.

---

## Driving Questions

Who is this for? What do they need from me? What does a professional submission actually look like when someone opens the folder?

---

## Goals and Objectives

**By the end of the unit, students will be able to:**
- Identify an opportunity that fits their practice and constraints
- Read a call for submissions and extract requirements, criteria, rights, and deadlines
- Edit a portfolio into a coherent submission sequence
- Write a clear cover letter and proposal in concrete language
- Assemble files correctly, ready for submission

## Assessments

**Formative: Opportunity Shortlist**
One page. Two opportunities with an eligibility and requirements checklist for each. Due at the start of Class 2.

**Summative: Submission Packet**
Complete and submission-ready. Proof of submission or a final package prepared for submission. Due end of Class 5.

---

## Submission Packet Checklist

- Chosen opportunity, link or brief listing screenshot
- Eligibility check and requirement notes, half page
- Artist statement, 150 to 250 words
- Project proposal or cover letter, 250 to 400 words tailored to the opportunity
- Portfolio PDF, 8 to 12 images with captions
- Documentation standards met, clean images and consistent formatting
- CV or activity list, one page appropriate to high school level
- File naming and formatting done professionally, single folder and consistent titles

## Formatting and Submission Standards

No decorative elements. No informal fonts. No unformatted filenames. Consistent margins, consistent caption format, consistent naming convention throughout. Every file in the packet should look like it came from the same person who thought before they clicked save.
