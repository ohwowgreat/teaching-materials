# Art Appreciation

Art Appreciation is a full-year course built around the Mnemosyne Atlas method of Aby Warburg. Rather than moving through art history in a single, fixed timeline, this course asks you to think across time, place, and medium: to find connections, patterns, and tensions between works that do not obviously belong together, and to build arguments from that constellation thinking.

The course runs in two semesters. Semester 1 is organized thematically, around the categories you will see in the unit list below. Semester 2 shifts toward the systems that shape how art is made, seen, valued, and distributed: patronage, markets, institutions, visibility, and professional practice. The year ends with a collaborative capstone project, an atlas-style book produced collectively by the class.

---

## What this course asks of you

You will look carefully, write precisely, and argue from evidence. You will be expected to explain not just what a formal choice means but how it acts on a viewer. You will build visual arguments, read challenging texts, and produce work that holds up to scrutiny. This is not a survey course. It is a thinking course.

The theorists and frameworks you will encounter include John Berger, Michael Baxandall, Pierre Bourdieu, Walter Benjamin, Susan Sontag, Jean Baudrillard, Vilém Flusser, Hito Steyerl, and Marshall McLuhan.

---

## Assessments

Work in this course is assessed through a combination of formative tasks (short responses, exercises, and in-class work) and summative tasks (formal essays, projects, and the capstone book). Summative tasks are graded on the strength of the argument, the precision of the formal analysis, and whether the writing proves its claims rather than simply asserting them.

## Course Outline

### Semester 1

- [Origins and Ancestral Memory](semester-1/01-origins-and-ancestral-memory.md) — Where images come from. Memory, survival, repetition, and the impulse to mark.
- [Narrative, Myth, and Allegory](semester-1/02-narrative-myth-and-allegory/README.md) — How stories structure images, and how images structure belief.
- [Food and Ethics](semester-1/03-food-and-ethics/README.md) — The still life as moral argument. Labor, abundance, and what consumption reveals.
- [Images, Mediation, and Modernity](semester-1/04-images-mediation-and-modernity/README.md) — Photography, reproduction, and what happens to images when they circulate at scale.

### Semester 2

- [Patronage and the Art Market](semester-2/patronage-and-the-art-market/README.md) — The eight parts of this unit move from early modern patronage through contemporary gallery systems, auction markets, visibility economies, and professional practice.
