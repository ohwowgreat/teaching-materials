# Lesson 3: A Messy Table, A Map of the World

**Focus work:** Willem Claesz Heda, *Still Life with a Gilt Cup*, 1635, Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam

**Goal for today:** Use close looking to show how a Dutch breakfast table can map trade, class, and value. Write one clear paragraph that makes a claim using visual and historical evidence.

## What you will do

1. **Look and label**
   - On your print, label every object you see.
   - Guess each material.
   - Circle any strong reflections or highlights.

2. **Trace the composition**
   - Place tracing paper over the image.
   - Draw the main triangle or triangles, the horizon line, and the strongest diagonals.
   - Note where Heda creates balance and where he creates near accidents.

3. **Discuss in a small group**
   - How does Heda balance order and instability.
   - Which two details feel most alive.
   - Share one observation that connects form to meaning.

4. **Connect to ethics**
   - You will receive a short quote from John Berger about value, possession, or seeing.
   - Link the quote to two concrete details in the painting.

5. **Write your paragraph**
   - 7 to 9 sentences.
   - Make a clear claim about how Heda makes the circulation of goods visible.
   - Use one formal detail and one trade or context fact.

## Helpful vocabulary

Pronkstilleven, vanitas, trompe l'oeil, facture, specular highlight, repoussoir, motif, commodity, circulation, balance, diagonal

## Quick tips

- Be specific. Name the object, name the material, describe what the light does.
- One strong claim is better than three weak ones.
- Use the vocabulary to be precise.

## Likely objects you may spot

Gilt standing cup, pewter plate, half peeled lemon, Römer glass, white bread, knife with mother of pearl, napkin, overturned beaker, salt cellar
