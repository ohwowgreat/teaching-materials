# Food and Ethics

> *"To eat is to appropriate, to take into ourselves the lives of others. The act of eating is not only the most basic of necessities, but also the most profound of moral acts."*
> — John Berger, *The Eaters and the Eaten*

This theme begins from Berger's observation that every act of nourishment carries an ethical dimension. To eat is to take life; to survive is to participate in a chain of consumption that reveals both interdependence and violence. Through art and film, we will explore how food becomes an image of morality, ritual, and desire; how the table, the feast, and the slaughterhouse each express the human condition.

## Essential Question

How does art use food to reveal what it means to live, share, and take from the world responsibly?

## Learning Goals

- Explain how artists represent food as a symbol of care, faith, or power.
- Connect Berger's idea of eating as a moral act to historical and modern artworks.
- Compare sacred, communal, and domestic images of the meal.
- Reflect on your own relationship with food, attention, and ethics.
- Present ideas through writing, discussion, or creative response.

## Key Ideas

Ethics, representation, labor, care, pleasure, transformation, sharing
