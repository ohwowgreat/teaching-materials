# 6a. Dialectics and Danto's End of Art

This stage formalizes "dialectic" as a method for reading art history: not a smooth progression of styles, but a sequence of tensions where each new image condition produces a response that transforms the field. The lesson(s) culminate in Danto's claim that the "end of art" is the end of a single historical narrative of necessary progress. Warhol becomes the case study where visual appearance no longer settles the question of art; interpretation, context, and the artworld do. This is the final turn from how images look to what images are.

## Goals and Objectives

Students will:
- Define dialectic as a way of describing historical change through tension and response.
- Apply dialectical thinking to the sequence photography → Impressionism → modernism → conceptual art.
- Explain Danto's "end of art" as a shift from stylistic necessity to philosophical pluralism.
- Use Warhol's *Brillo Boxes* to articulate the difference between visual equivalence and conceptual distinction.

## Key Questions

- What does dialectic explain that a simple timeline of styles cannot?
- When appearance stops being decisive, what replaces it: concept, context, institution, theory?
- What does Danto mean by "end of art," and why is Warhol the turning point?
- How does this help explain why images look the way they do now?

## Core Dialectics

- Representation vs autonomy
- Object vs idea
- Visual difference vs interpretive difference
- History as progress vs history as pluralism

## Works and Texts Covered

- Andy Warhol, *Brillo Boxes* (1964)
- Kazimir Malevich, *Black Square* (1915) — prefiguration of the break from depiction
- Marcel Duchamp, *Fountain* (1917) — redefinition device
- John Baldessari, photo + text works — reading images as propositions
- Danto: key ideas from *The End of Art* / "Artworld" framing
- Reference points as needed: Benjamin (reproduction), Baudrillard (image-world), Steyerl (circulation / hierarchy)
