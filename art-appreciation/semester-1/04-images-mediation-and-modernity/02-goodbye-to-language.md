# 2. Goodbye to Language

**Film:** *Goodbye to Language*, dir. Jean-Luc Godard, 2014

## Film Screening: Guiding Questions

- How does the film treat perception? Are the images stable, or constantly splitting, shifting, and glitching?
- When the image breaks (3D split, overlap, digital noise), what happens to your sense of reality in the scene?
- Does the dog's point of view feel different from the human scenes? In what way?
- Which aspects of your experience come from the content (characters, fragments of dialogue, quotes) and which from the medium (3D, digital texture, editing, sound, resolution)?

---

## Marshall McLuhan – "The Medium Is the Message"

- The form of a medium (film, television, smartphone video, 3D, social media) has more lasting impact than any specific content it carries.
- The "message" of a medium is how it reorganizes experience, the senses, and social relations:
  - Print encourages linear, sequential thinking.
  - Film and television create a continuous flow of images and sound.
  - Digital media normalize fragmentation, multitasking, and constant connectivity.
- What matters is not only *what* a film says, but *what kind of seeing and feeling* the film's medium trains in its viewers.

**Key idea for the film:** *Goodbye to Language* turns its medium (digital 3D cinema) into the main message: split stereoscopy, unstable focus, and layered sound draw attention to the device and format themselves, showing how contemporary image technology reshapes perception and communication.

---

## John Berger – Ways of Seeing (Chapter 1)

- Chapter 1 of *Ways of Seeing* argues that images are not neutral windows; each image is constructed from a particular viewpoint.
- "Seeing comes before words," but what is seen is shaped by knowledge, habit, and culture.
- Reproduction and context change the meaning of images: the "same" image behaves differently in a church, a museum, a book, an advertisement, or on a phone screen.
- Images do not just show things; they organize how we look at them, and can be arranged to persuade, sell, or frame a particular way of seeing the world.

**Key idea for the film:** Every shot embodies a way of seeing. In *Goodbye to Language*, unusual framings, overlaid text, and broken 3D depth make those ways of seeing more visible and unstable, rather than hiding them behind smooth storytelling.

---

## Hito Steyerl – The Poor Image

- The "poor image" is low-resolution, compressed, pirated, glitched – an image that has lost quality but gained speed and reach.
- Poor images circulate online, copied and re-uploaded, often detached from their original context and turned into fragments.
- The hierarchy between high-quality cinema images and cheap digital scraps is questioned; both belong to the same global image economy.
- Poor images reveal how power, access, and platforms shape which images survive, which get upgraded, and which remain degraded.

**Key idea for the film:** Degraded digital images can be expressive and critical, not just broken. *Goodbye to Language* mixes crisp 3D with ugly pixelation, blown-out color, and digital artifacts, placing "poor images" inside an art-film context and forcing us to think about what kind of image quality we trust or value.
