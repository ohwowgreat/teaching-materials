# Manet / Titian Comparison

**Purpose:** Rehearse how to defend the claim using precise visual evidence.
**Time:** 30–45 minutes
**Materials:** Both images projected side by side. The quote on screen.

## Board setup (write this before students arrive)

- Center: **"Manet confronts the tradition; Titian invited it." — Berger**
- Left column header: **INVITES** (Titian)
- Right column header: **CONFRONTS** (Manet)

## Opening move (3–5 min)

Ask one question and let silence work:

**"If you had to prove Berger right with a single visual detail from each painting, what would you choose and why?"**

Take 2–4 quick volunteer answers.

## Build the argument in four axes (20–25 min)

Use these as talk prompts. For each axis, get at least one detail for each painting on the board. Keep pushing for "detail → effect on the viewer."

1. **Address to the viewer**
   - Titian invites: lowered or gentle gaze, softened posture, receptive staging.
   - Manet confronts: direct eye contact, self-possessed expression, the viewer is seen and sized up.
   - **Push:** "What happens to the power dynamic when the figure looks back?"

2. **Myth vs. modernity**
   - Titian invites: mythic Venus, marriage symbols (cassone, roses, myrtle), a story that domesticates desire.
   - Manet confronts: a contemporary woman named "Olympia," client's bouquet, a Black maid delivering it, the black cat.
   - **Push:** "What does replacing myth with contemporary cues do to the viewer's comfort?"

3. **Gesture and the hand**
   - Titian invites: relaxed, guiding hand, open curves that lead the eye.
   - Manet confronts: firm, blocking hand, planar body, withheld access.
   - **Push:** "If the hand is a thesis statement, state the thesis of each hand in one sentence."

4. **Light, space, and paint handling**
   - Titian invites: atmospheric depth, caressing light, colorito that flatters flesh.
   - Manet confronts: compressed space, stark value breaks, edges that expose surface.
   - **Push:** "Does the paint want to please you or to wake you up? Cite a passage."

## Evidence bank to seed the room (use only as needed)

- **Dog vs. cat:** sleeping dog of fidelity vs. alert black cat that signals charge and vigilance.
- **Attendants:** domestic maids at a cassone vs. Laure delivering a timed bouquet.
- **Titles:** "Venus" as mythic cover vs. "Olympia" as a known pseudonym in 19th-century Paris.
- **Backgrounds:** palazzo depth that calms the gaze vs. tight bourgeois room that confronts it.

## Socratic follow-ups that raise the level

- "Name the tradition. What, exactly, is being invited in Titian and confronted in Manet?"
- "Where does Titian still feel bold or even confrontational to you? Where does Manet still invite? Make the case for one exception on each side."
- "How do race and class become legible in Manet, and how does that change the viewer's role?"
- "If Titian writes a gracious invitation, what are the terms printed on it? If Manet delivers a confrontation, what is the demand?"

## Likely misconceptions and quick redirects

- **"They are the same pose, so same meaning."**
  Redirect: "Same pose, different function. What work does myth perform in Titian that Manet strips away?"
- **"Manet paints worse."**
  Redirect: "Name the purpose of the flatness and the edge. What does it do to the spectator's comfort?"
- **"The maid is background."**
  Redirect: "If you remove the maid and bouquet, what social reading collapses?"

## High-yield student phrasing to model in the room

- "Titian **invites** by staging a viewer who is expected and reassured."
- "Manet **confronts** by addressing the viewer directly and refusing mythic cover."
- "The substitution of a bouquet for myrtle and roses shifts the scene from marriage to transaction."

## Tight closing (3–5 min)

Round-robin: each student states one sentence that begins with **"Titian invites by…"** or **"Manet confronts by…"** and includes a concrete visual detail. No repeats allowed.
