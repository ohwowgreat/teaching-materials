# 5. Photography

## Overview

This lesson traces how photography reshaped art and visual culture. We will look at how painting changed once cameras could record the world, and how photographs later became central to modern life, shaping memory, truth, identity, and what feels real.

---

## Topics Covered

- The invention of photography and why it mattered for art
- Impressionism and the "photographic" way of seeing (cropping, snapshots, modern life)
- Photography, truth, evidence, and manipulation (edited media, Instagram)
- Susan Sontag's ideas about photography and the ethics of seeing
- Two big directions we will follow next:
  1. How painting changed after photography
  2. How photography changed the social landscape and layered images over reality (Baudrillard)

---

## Key Questions

- What did photography change about what painting is for?
- Why do photographs often feel more "true" than paintings?
- What is lost when the world becomes saturated with images?
- How do images shape what feels important, memorable, or real?
- When does an image become more powerful than the event itself?

---

## Key Concepts

- **Framing / Cropping:** what is included and excluded in an image
- **Snapshot aesthetic:** the look of a captured moment (not staged, off-center, partial)
- **Image saturation:** living in an environment flooded with pictures
- **Ethics of seeing:** how looking can involve power, control, and responsibility
- **Pseudo-presence:** photographs make something feel present even when it is absent
- **Evidence:** photographs seem like proof, even when they distort or are edited

## Lesson Timeline

**0–5 min | Do Now**
A single image will be on the screen. Write a few sentences:
- What do you see?
- What does it make you think of?
- Does it feel like a document, an artwork, or both?

**5–10 min | Discussion**
We share a few responses and identify what made the image feel "real" or "constructed."

**10–20 min | Painting and Photography**
We compare a pre-photography style painting with Impressionist painting to see how painting changes once the camera exists.

**20–30 min | Photography as Power and Social Practice**
We look at examples (edited magazine cover, Instagram post, contemporary art photography) to see how photos shape identity, belief, and attention.

**30–40 min | Guided Discussion**
We connect what we see to Sontag's claims about photographs as evidence, objects, and tools of power.

**40–45 min | Reflection**
Write 3–4 sentences: **How does photography change what we value, remember, and believe?**
